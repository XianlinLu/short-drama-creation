# Original Story Workflow

## Intake and planning

Extract only what the user actually supplied: desired result, premise, characters, audience, length, genre, emotional payoff, forbidden elements, reference signals, and whether research is requested.

When the premise can support several valid stories, form four direction bundles and use `interaction-and-topic-ui.md`. Stop after the native card.

When one direction has been selected, produce a compact planning checkpoint unless the user explicitly requests immediate drafting:

```text
[why this version will hold attention]
[opening disturbance]
[protagonist's immediate want]
[pressure relationship]
[three or more escalations]
[turning point or reveal]
[ending consequence]
[request for confirmation]
```

Planning and full drafting belong to separate executions when confirmation is requested. Silence is not approval.

## Story construction

Select a small combination of engines that reinforce one another. Examples of reusable functions include hidden capability, mistaken identity, costly bargain, missing memory, public test, deadline, procedural trap, object evidence, rival alliance, forbidden access, or status reversal. Give every engine a cost or constraint.

Build causal movement:

```text
disturbance → choice → resistance → changed stakes → costly action → reversal → consequence
```

For short work, compress setup but not causality. A new scene must change knowledge, leverage, danger, relationship, or available choices. If removing a scene changes nothing, combine or cut it.

## Reader promise by genre

- **Suspense:** clues are visible before the solution; each answer changes the question; the reveal reinterprets earlier evidence.
- **Romance:** attraction and resistance both affect choices; vulnerability costs something; the ending resolves the emotional contract.
- **Comeback or revenge:** small proofs arrive before the major reversal; victory follows earned evidence and timing; success creates a new consequence.
- **Fantasy or progression:** rules appear through pressure; gains have limits; advancement changes strategy rather than only power level.
- **Science fiction:** one speculative premise shapes daily behavior and the climax; human choice matters more than explanation.
- **Workplace or institutional satire:** every faction has a concrete incentive and lever; procedure changes outcomes in scene; the protagonist still makes a meaningful moral choice.
- **Children's story:** threat and vocabulary fit the audience; conflict is legible; wonder is carried by action and image.

## Openings, dialogue, and images

The opening should make the literal situation understandable while withholding one productive answer. Start with a broken expectation, irreversible action, visible anomaly, or urgent decision. Avoid a poetic sentence whose grammar suggests an impossible event unless the next line immediately clarifies it.

Dialogue should do at least two jobs: pursue a goal, hide information, change status, apply pressure, reveal a relationship, or force a choice. Remove exchanges that merely restate facts both speakers know.

Use recurring objects and locations as causal devices. A prop should record evidence, limit action, trigger memory, alter trust, or return with changed meaning.

## Reference and research handling

If the user names a work or creator, identify abstract functions such as pacing density, investigation structure, social pressure, emotional distance, or visual rhythm. Recombine those functions with new characters, worlds, plot logic, scenes, language, and motifs.

Do not imitate a living creator's distinctive voice. Convert style requests into general craft parameters. Do not reproduce recognizable dialogue, characters, branded worlds, signature props, iconic scenes, or a distinctive sequence of beats.

Search only when the user asks for current or source-backed research and a connected action exists. Record public facts and high-level narrative functions, separate fact from interpretation, then create new direction bundles. If more than one bundle is offered, use the native selector.

## Revision route

Classify feedback before editing:

- comprehension or opening ambiguity;
- plot causality or structure;
- pacing and scene economy;
- motivation or relationship pressure;
- dialogue and voice;
- imagery and motif;
- climax or ending;
- language texture.

Repair the narrowest layer that solves the problem. Keep established names, facts, point of view, tense, and successful scenes unless the requested repair depends on changing them. For large structural changes, show a revised plan before rewriting unless the user explicitly asks for the full revision now.

After meaningful user feedback, identify whether the failure came from routing, planning, causality, scene execution, language, media continuity, or action handling. Extract a reusable rule only when it applies beyond the current example; keep one-off preferences attached to the current work instead of turning them into universal behavior.

## Delivery check

Before returning a complete story, confirm:

- the protagonist wants something observable;
- conflict escalates through at least three changed conditions when length permits;
- the climax depends on earlier choices or evidence;
- dialogue carries pressure or subtext;
- concrete images influence action;
- the ending leaves a consequence, decision, or reinterpretation;
- language does not fall into repeated stock constructions;
- the output is complete at the requested length.

For a requested demonstration or craft review, return localized sections equivalent to Input, Technique Mix, Story Body, and Creation Self-Review. Keep internal diagnostics out of ordinary story delivery.
